How to read the totals. "Ordered so far" is what the five orders cost. Delivery was free except for the eye glasses, 3DJake took off 1 percent, and the glass seller refunds USD 5, which I counted as about CHF 4. "Still to buy" takes the cheaper source on every open line for the low figure and the dearer one for the high figure. Euro prices are converted at 0.95 francs, which is my assumption, and the three allowances are my estimates.

Why the Pi went from 4 GB to 8 GB

Version 1 of this plan chose the 4 GB board because US list prices put the 8 GB board at about double. Swiss shop prices are much closer: CHF 129.00 for 4 GB at Bastelgarage against CHF 164.90 for 8 GB at pi-shop.ch.

Everything this plan runs on the Pi fits in 4 GB. The extra CHF 36 buys room to run speech to text on the robot itself, so it keeps working when my laptop is closed. More memory does not make the Pi fast enough to host the language model. That stays off the robot.

Why a power-tool battery

The original design uses a bare three-cell LiPo pouch that needs a balance charger and a fire bag. In a house with a small child I want a hard case and a charger that is a dock. A Bosch Professional 12 V pack also has three lithium-ion cells, so the voltage matches. Two packs and the fast charger cost CHF 87.90, about CHF 18 more than the LiPo, its charger and the bag.

I do not like a tool battery in a toy, and I looked at power banks, camera batteries and the DJI RoboMaster battery as well. Then I saw another builder run his robot on this Bosch pack, in a sleeve behind a rear flap. None of the others was easier to swap or safer for her to handle, so I ordered the Bosch set on 27 November.

Two caveats remain. Bosch makes these packs for its own tools only, so using one in a robot is at my own risk. And the robot has to bring its own fuse and low-voltage cutoff.
