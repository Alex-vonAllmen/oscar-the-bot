Changes to the design

Oscar starts from chillibasket's Wall-E replica. We use community remixes where other builders already solved a problem, and change the design ourselves where our electronics need room.

The camera eye, measured

Measured from chillibasket's assembly file on 16 November. The glass lens sits in a 30 mm opening. A camera lens 10 mm behind that opening can see through a cone of about 113 degrees, 18 mm behind it about 80 degrees, 22 mm behind it about 69 degrees.

The standard Camera Module 3 sees 75 degrees across the diagonal, so it clears the opening if it sits within about 20 mm of it. The wide version needs to sit almost against the glass. We use the standard one.

A plano-convex lens in front of a camera works as a close-up lens: nothing farther away than its focal length comes into focus. A binocular lens focuses at roughly 10 to 15 cm. So the camera eye gets a flat clear window, and only the other eye gets a glass lens. That is reasoning, not yet tested.

Does everything fit inside?

On paper, yes. The space below the neck servos is 129.5 mm wide, 94.7 mm high and 117 mm deep. Our component boxes and a reserve for a Pi add-on board fill 57 percent of it, with no collisions. The rest has to take the wiring, the mounts and the airflow, which the model does not show.

The tight spots: 1.4 mm between the Arduino stack and the servos above it, and 2 mm between the back wall and the boards. No connector can face the back.

This model puts the battery on the floor behind the front door. It was made before the rear flap idea, so the next version moves the battery to the back. The files are on the downloads page.

What we take from another builder

mneuhaus built a heavily reworked Wall-E for his WALL-E-DORA software. I looked at his photos and his public CAD model on Onshape, and measured the model against chillibasket's parts.

  • We take: the battery behind a rear flap, the power button on the front, and later the driven arms and hands.
  • We leave: the screens in the eyes.
  • Same size, different joints. His body has chillibasket's outside size and inside width, but his front and back panels sit between the side panels instead of reaching into them. His panels cannot be swapped in one at a time. The shell would have to go as a set.
  • His front button is a 12 mm latching metal switch, so it switches power on and off.
  • His track links are the same size as the TPU links we chose. He uses 34 per side.

We do not republish his model. Where we adapt one of his parts, it keeps his licence and credits him.

Our own parts

Two parts are ours to design: the front panel with the power button, and the rear flap with the battery sleeve. Neither is designed yet. They will be drawn with CadQuery against chillibasket's STEP files, like the fit model, and published as STEP and STL on the downloads page.
