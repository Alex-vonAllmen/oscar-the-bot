Build log

What happened, when, and what went wrong. Photos show Oscar and his parts, never my daughter.

24 Nov 2026

A battery flap and a power button, from another builder

I went through the photos and the public CAD model of mneuhaus's WALL-E-DORA robot. His battery is a Bosch 12 V tool pack in a sleeve behind a flap in the back panel, and it pulls out to the rear. That is now the direction for Oscar, though not yet a final decision.

Two more things come along: a button on the front panel that switches him on, and, after mission 13, arms and hands that move at more joints. Screens in the eyes stay out.

His body has the same outside size as chillibasket's, but the panels join differently, so they cannot be swapped in one at a time. Details are on the design changes page.

24 Nov 2026

Oscar has a name on this site

She named the robot Oscar. Until today this site only said "the robot". From now on it uses his name, and the name plate she prints in mission 1 will say OSCAR.

The site itself is now split into pages: the plan, the parts, our design changes, downloads, this log and the credits.

23 Nov 2026

Printer and first parts ordered

The printer is a Bambu Lab P2S Combo: enclosed, with Bambu's AMS filament unit. It cost CHF 568, against the CHF 250 the parts list had for the open-frame A1.

With it went the filament, including soft TPU for the track test, and most of what missions 1 to 9 need: Pi, camera, servos, Arduino, motor shield, servo driver, distance sensors, game controller, speaker and amplifier, and the bits for the desk rigs. Three orders, CHF 1,170.70 in total. The parts list now shows what was paid. Mission 1 starts when the printer arrives, which should be early December.

16 Nov 2026

Everything fits inside, on paper

I measured the free space inside the body from chillibasket's assembly file and placed a box for each part of our electronics: Pi, battery, Arduino stack, converter, servo driver, speaker and amplifier. Nothing collides, and the boxes fill 57 percent of the space below the neck servos. The tightest gap is 1.4 mm.

16 Nov 2026

The plan turned around: head first, body last

My first plan printed and painted every part before anything moved. That is a month of printing with nothing for her to do. The plan is now thirteen short missions. Each one ends with something that works: an arm that waves, an eye that finds her face, a head that looks sad. Camera, voice and the teach game run on the desk long before the robot drives. The price is that I wire the head twice.

16 Nov 2026

A game controller instead of a phone

The main control is now a Bluetooth game controller paired with the Pi, so driving needs no phone, browser or Wi-Fi. The web interface stays for setup. One controller button becomes push-to-talk. The robot has to stop when the controller connection drops. New estimate: CHF 975 to 1,145 with the printer.

15 Nov 2026

Which remixes we use

Read the community remixes listed on chillibasket's page and picked six: soft TPU tracks, wheel frames with track tension and bearings, a drive gear held by a grub screw, a head bolted to the neck, printed eye linkages and magnetic door holders. The tracks still have to win a floor test in mission 8. The list with links and licences is on the design changes page.

15 Nov 2026

Tool battery, distance sensors, microphone

Compared the plan with what is known about Microduck's hardware. Two things carried over: a hard-cased removable battery and distance sensing. The LiPo is replaced by a Bosch 12 V tool battery, and two distance sensors on the Arduino now stop the robot at walls and edges. The microphone is settled on a simple USB one with a physical mute. New estimate: CHF 940 to 1,100 with the printer.

15 Nov 2026

Parts priced, Pi upgraded

Priced every line of the parts list, mostly in Swiss shops. The estimate is CHF 920 to 1,100 with the printer and CHF 670 to 850 without. Switched the Pi from 4 GB to 8 GB after finding the Swiss price gap is about CHF 36, and moved speech to text onto the robot.

14 Nov 2026

Plan published

Chose a tracked robot over a walking one, dropped reinforcement learning from the main build, and split the AI into three layers she can each use on her own. Nothing is printed and nothing is ordered yet.
